# Adversarial / Ensemble / Multi-Model LLM Approaches: Evidence Report (through June 2026)

## 1. Multi-agent debate (MAD): foundational claims and the correction

- **Du et al. 2023** ("Improving Factuality and Reasoning…", ICML 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325) showed 3 agents × 2 debate rounds improved math/strategic reasoning and factuality over single-model and self-reflection baselines across GSM8K, biographies, chess.
- **Degeneration-of-Thought (DoT)**: Liang et al. (EMNLP 2024) coined DoT — once a model commits to an answer it cannot generate novel self-corrections; MAD with a devil's-advocate "tit-for-tat" structure was proposed as the fix (https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/multi-agent-debate-mad-strategies).
- **The 2025–26 correction is significant.** "Stop Overvaluing Multi-Agent Debate" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08788) finds that under **matched token budgets, MAD does not consistently beat self-consistency** across MMLU/GSM8K/MATH/HumanEval/MBPP — much of debate's apparent gain is just extra compute. "Talk Isn't Always Cheap" (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05396v1) shows debate can actively hurt: **correct→incorrect flips outnumber incorrect→correct flips**; e.g., −5.0% on CommonSenseQA (3 Mistral agents), −6.8% GSM8K (2 Llama + 1 Mistral), up to **−12%** in some heterogeneous configs; degradation worsens over rounds. A controlled logical-reasoning study (https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07784v1) similarly finds MAD often redundant or harmful — it can override an initially correct single-agent answer.
- **Net pattern**: debate helps most on math/multi-step reasoning where errors are heterogeneous and verifiable in-dialogue; it helps least (or hurts) on commonsense/knowledge tasks where agents share correlated priors and conform.

## 2. Mixture-of-Agents and the homogeneity surprise

- **MoA (Wang et al., ICLR 2025 Spotlight)**: layered architecture (default 3 layers × 6 proposers + aggregator); each layer sees prior-layer outputs as auxiliary context. Open-source-only MoA hit **65.1–65.7% LC win on AlpacaEval 2.0, beating GPT-4o** (https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/5434be94e82c54327bb9dcaf7fca52b6-Paper-Conference.pdf).
- **Self-MoA rebuttal** ("Rethinking Mixture-of-Agents", https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00674v1): sampling the **single best model repeatedly beats mixing different models** — +6.6 pts on AlpacaEval 2.0 (65.7 vs 59.1), +3.8% avg on MMLU/CRUX/MATH. Regression shows **quality coefficient ≫ diversity coefficient** (CRUX: 4.55 vs 1.42). Mixing wins only when models are near-parity with complementary strengths, and then only marginally (+0.17–0.35%).

## 3. Self-consistency, best-of-N, and test-time compute

- **Self-consistency** remains a brutal baseline: SOTA verifier pipelines can need **8× more compute to match it**, with marginal gains even at 128× budget (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14913); hybrid discriminative verification adds up to +5.1% on AIME2025 at ~2% overhead.
- **"More Agents Is All You Need"** (Li et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05120v1): pure sampling-and-voting scales monotonically with agent count; gains correlate with task difficulty.
- **Snell et al. 2024**: compute-optimal test-time scaling (adaptive verifier search/revision) beats parameter scaling; smaller models + inference compute can outperform much larger models.
- **External verification is the load-bearing element**: Reflexion (verbal RL with environment/test feedback) reached **91% pass@1 HumanEval vs GPT-4's 80%** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366); Self-Refine helps on style/preference tasks (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17651). But **Huang et al. ICLR'24** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798) showed **intrinsic self-correction without external feedback fails and often degrades reasoning accuracy**; "LLMs cannot find reasoning errors, but can correct them given the error location" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.08516) localizes the bottleneck to error *detection*, not repair. SELF-[IN]CORRECT (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.04298) shows models discriminate their own generations worse than they generate. **Kambhampati's LLM-Modulo** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01817): LLMs can't self-verify plans; LLM-generates + bank-of-external-critics verifies surpasses 78% human-level on Blocksworld with GPT-4/Claude.

## 4. LLM-as-judge reliability

- **Self-preference bias**: GPT-4 shows ~**+10% win-rate inflation for its own outputs**, mechanistically tied to low perplexity / self-recognition (Panickssery et al. 2024; Wataoka et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819). Family bias (same-provider favoritism) is also documented.
- **Position and verbosity bias** persist across model families; frontier models fail >50% of bias stress tests (https://www.adaline.ai/blog/llm-as-a-judge-reliability-bias).
- **Mitigations with evidence**: swap orderings and average (position); reference-guided grading; multi-judge ensembles from **different providers than the generator**; calibration against human spot-checks (Gu et al. 2025 survey, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666675825004564). **ChatEval** (ICLR 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07201): multi-persona referee debate aligns with humans better than single-judge — diverse personas are essential; homogeneous-role debate adds little.

## Direct answers to the five questions

**(1) Does sending one model's plan to 2–3 other models for critique help?** Yes, measurably — *when critiques are grounded*. Iterative Consensus Ensemble (3 cross-provider LLMs critiquing to consensus) gains **+7–15 pts over best single model** on reasoning/medical benchmarks; cross-model code review catches complementary bug classes per architecture (https://zylos.ai/research/2026-02-17-multi-model-ai-code-review). Trained critics (CTRL, https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03492v1) give up to 106% relative improvement on code. Strongest gains: code (executable feedback), math, constraint-checkable planning (LLM-Modulo). Weakest/negative: commonsense, open-ended judgment tasks where critique induces conformity (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05396v1). Critique-style structured feedback beats free-form debate.

**(2) Optimal critics/rounds?** Convergent finding: **3 agents (2–4 range), 2–3 rounds**; accuracy plateaus then *declines* from repetition, context overload, and error propagation (https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00066v1; https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/multi-agent-debate-approach). One study: 72%→87% from 1→3 agents, 90% at 5, ~flat beyond. Adaptive early-stop ("adaptive break"; iMAD triggers debate only when the single answer looks uncertain, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11306) beats fixed schedules.

**(3) Diversity vs same-model sampling?** Contested; reconcile by quality gap. If one model is clearly strongest, **Self-MoA/homogeneous sampling of that model wins** (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00674v1). Among **near-parity frontier models, heterogeneity wins decisively**: 2 diverse agents match 16 homogeneous (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03794); homogeneous scaling collapses to zero marginal gain; heterogeneity also de-correlates judge bias. Caveat: heterogeneous debate has the worst documented sycophancy cascades — diversity must be paired with structured critique, not free agreement.

**(4) Who aggregates?** A **separate selector/judge beats planner self-incorporation**. "When Agents Disagree" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20324) shows self-selection is a bottleneck — agents prefer their own outputs regardless of quality; dedicated external selection consistently wins. Self-preference bias (+10%) directly contaminates planner-judged loops. MoA uses a single strong aggregator as final layer. Practical pattern: planner revises against critiques (models *can* fix located errors), but a **different-provider judge** decides acceptance/convergence.

**(5) Failure modes & mitigations.** (a) **Sycophantic convergence** — stronger models abandon correct answers to agree with weaker peers (RLHF-induced); correct→incorrect flips dominate (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05396v1; https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02668). (b) **Majority-wrong cascades** — confidently-wrong agents flip the group; longer debates amplify (https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06426). (c) **Conformity attacks** — MAD-Spear prompt injection exploits conformity (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13038). (d) **Problem drift** over rounds (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.19559). Mitigations with evidence: communicate **confidence/uncertainty** (DebUnc attention-scaled confidence beats vanilla debate); independent first drafts before any cross-exposure; structured critique requiring justification, penalizing unjustified agreement; expertise-weighted votes; external verifiers/tools as ground truth (Tool-MAD, LLM-Modulo); judge ≠ generator provider; trigger debate selectively only on uncertain cases (iMAD).

**Bottom line for a plan-critique pipeline**: 2–3 near-parity cross-provider critics, 1–2 critique-revise rounds, independent critiques (no cross-talk before drafts), planner revises, separate different-provider judge accepts — with any available executable/constraint verification dominating all model-only signals.
